# Reusable Cold-Email Drafting Guidelines

## Purpose

Generate concise, evidence-based outreach that connects:

1. one verified fact about the recipient or company;
2. one relevant capability from the sender's uploaded résumé or saved profile; and
3. one direct, low-friction call to action.

The system must work for different users, industries, seniority levels, and campaign goals. It must never rely on a built-in persona.

## Source hierarchy

Use information in this order:

1. saved user profile;
2. uploaded résumé;
3. user-saved campaign instructions;
4. verified company research;
5. contact metadata.

If sources conflict, the saved user profile wins. If a fact is unavailable, omit it. Never invent experience, employers, credentials, results, location, rates, or availability.

## Drafting workflow

### 1. Understand the recipient

Identify the recipient's role, company, likely decision context, and whether they are:

- a direct buyer;
- a recruiter or placement channel;
- a hiring manager;
- a potential partner; or
- another relevant stakeholder.

### 2. Select one company signal

Use one specific, observable signal from the website, product, hiring activity, public operating model, or supplied research. Do not claim knowledge of private systems.

### 3. Select matching sender evidence

Choose the strongest fact from the uploaded résumé or profile that is relevant to the signal. Relevance matters more than listing credentials.

### 4. Explain the connection

State the practical problem or opportunity created by the company signal, then connect it to what the sender has actually done.

### 5. Ask directly

End with one short question. Use the user's saved CTA when provided; otherwise ask whether a brief conversation or referral to the correct person makes sense.

## Structure

Hi [First name],

[Specific, verified company or role signal.]

[One plausible challenge or opportunity created by that signal.]

[One or two first-person sentences using verified sender experience.]

[One direct question.]

Best,
[Sender name]

## Style rules

- Write in the sender's first-person voice.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Default to 100–160 words unless the user requests otherwise.
- Avoid hype, generic compliments, buzzwords, and résumé dumping.
- Do not mention AI, automation, prompts, or internal scoring.
- Do not use a fact merely because it sounds impressive; it must be relevant.
- Never write placeholders such as `undefined` or unresolved braces.
- Do not include a signature name unless the profile provides one.

## Campaign adaptations

### Direct buyer

Connect an observable business need to verified sender experience and a credible offer.

### Recruiting firm

Treat the recruiter as a placement or introduction channel unless the campaign explicitly sells to the firm. Make the sender's verified role fit scannable.

### Startup

Anchor to a product, customer, technical, hiring, or growth signal. Do not invent the internal stack.

### Regulated industry

Respect compliance and risk sensitivity. Mention regulated experience only when the résumé supports it.

## Quality rubric

Score each draft on:

- specificity of recipient research;
- commercial relevance;
- evidence-based sender positioning;
- use of verified résumé/profile facts;
- clarity of the call to action;
- brevity and readability; and
- absence of invented claims.

Reject or regenerate drafts that fail source grounding, contain generic praise, overstate the sender's background, or could be sent unchanged to another company.
